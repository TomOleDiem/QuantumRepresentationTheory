Definition 45Angular momentum representation

An algebraic angular momentum representation is an explicit representation \(\rho \) (Definition 7) of the Lie algebra \(\mathfrak {so}_3 \cong \mathfrak {sl}_2\) on a state space \(V\), presented via operators \(J_x, J_y, J_z\) satisfying \([J_x,J_y] = iJ_z\) and cyclic permutations. The physical requirement that \(J_x,J_y,J_z\) be self-adjoint on a Hilbert space is not part of this definition: it is exactly the kind of fact that belongs on the Physlib side of the future bridge discussed in the Overview, not something assumed here.

Caveat: the commutation relations here are currently axiomatized as three fields of a structure. Mathlib already packages \(\mathfrak {so}_3\) concretely as Cross.lieRing, the Lie ring structure on \(\texttt{Fin 3} \to R\) given by the cross product (in Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.CrossProduct), with the Jacobi identity and antisymmetry already proved (jacobi_cross, cross_anticomm). Presenting angular momentum as an honest representation of that concrete Lie ring, rather than an axiomatized triple of operators, is a more idiomatic route not yet taken — see the caveats chapter.

Definition 37Basis-dependent Casimir element

For a representation \(\rho \) of \(L\) on \(V\), a nondegenerate and Lie-invariant form \(\Phi \) (Definition 35), and a basis \(x_1,\dots ,x_d\) of \(L\) with \(\Phi \)-dual basis \(x^1,\dots ,x^d\), the Casimir operator is

\[ C_\rho \; =\; \sum _{i=1}^d \rho (x_i)\, \rho (x^i) \; \in \; \operatorname {End}_K(V). \]

Invariance of \(\Phi \) is essential here, not just nondegeneracy: it is what makes Theorem 40 true.

Definition 36Dual basis

Definition 35Nondegenerate invariant bilinear form

A bilinear form \(\Phi \) on \(L\) is Lie-invariant if \(\Phi ([x,y],z) + \Phi (y,[x,z]) = 0\) for all \(x,y,z \in L\); it is nondegenerate if its radical is trivial. For \(L\) semisimple (over a characteristic-zero field, e.g. \(\mathbb {C}\)), the Killing form is such a form: Mathlib has both Cartan criteria in Mathlib.Algebra.Lie.CartanCriterion, and in particular LieAlgebra.hasTrivialRadical_iff_isKilling gives the equivalence between trivial radical (which coincides with semisimplicity in characteristic zero) and a nondegenerate Killing form (LieAlgebra.IsKilling).

Definition 7Explicit representation

An explicit representation of \(L\) on \(V\) is a Lie algebra homomorphism \(\rho \colon L \to \operatorname {End}_K(V)\). This is only needed for the physics layer (Chapters 4–5), where the same underlying type must carry several differently-sourced actions (e.g. two named \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-triples on one bound state space) without polluting the global instance cache with competing LieModule instances. It packages the same data as Definition 2, presented explicitly instead of via typeclass search.

Theorem 48Angular momentum generates an \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-triple

If \(J_z \neq 0\), then \((J_z, J_+, J_-)\) is an \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-triple in the sense of Definition 18, via the bridge construction of Remark 13 turning \(\rho \) into local Lie-module instances.

The hypothesis \(J_z \neq 0\) is necessary, not a formalization artifact: the all-zero representation (\(J_x=J_y=J_z=0\)) satisfies Definition 45 but gives \(h = 2J_z = 0\), which an \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-triple explicitly excludes. This is the honest statement of a real fact — the trivial, spin-\(0\) representation does not generate an actual copy of \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) inside \(\operatorname {End}(V)\) — and it means Theorem 49 and Theorem 50 downstream will need to either carry this hypothesis or special-case spin \(0\) separately.
