3 The Casimir element

Physically, “total angular momentum squared,” \(J^2 = J_x^2+J_y^2+J_z^2\), is special because it commutes with every rotation generator, so it can be diagonalized simultaneously with a full set of symmetry generators and used to label irreducible multiplets by a single quantum number \(j(j+1)\). The Casimir element is the algebraic definition making this precise for an arbitrary Lie algebra with an invariant form, not just \(\mathfrak {so}_3\): an element built from the generators themselves that is guaranteed to commute with all of them, and which Schur’s lemma (Chapter 1) then forces to act as a scalar on any irreducible representation. Chapter 4 identifies \(J^2\) with the \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) Casimir element built here, and Chapter 5 uses two Casimir elements at once — one for each of the two commuting \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\)-actions hiding inside the hydrogen atom’s dynamical symmetry — to pin down its spectrum.

A bilinear form \(\Phi \) on \(L\) is Lie-invariant if \(\Phi ([x,y],z) + \Phi (y,[x,z]) = 0\) for all \(x,y,z \in L\); it is nondegenerate if its radical is trivial. (For \(L\) semisimple over \(\mathbb {C}\), the Killing form is such a form.)

Given a basis \(x_1, \dots , x_d\) of \(L\) and a nondegenerate form \(\Phi \), the dual basis \(x^1, \dots , x^d\) is the unique basis with \(\Phi (x_i, x^j) = \delta _i^j\). (Standard linear-algebra fact about nondegenerate bilinear forms, available generically in Mathlib.)

Definition 37 Basis-dependent Casimir element

For a representation \(\rho \) of \(L\) on \(V\) and a basis \(x_1,\dots ,x_d\) of \(L\) with \(\Phi \)-dual basis \(x^1,\dots ,x^d\), the Casimir operator is

\[ C_\rho \; =\; \sum _{i=1}^d \rho (x_i)\, \rho (x^i) \; \in \; \operatorname {End}_K(V). \]

The universal enveloping algebra \(U(L)\), together with \(\iota \colon L \to U(L)\), has the universal property that every representation \(\rho \colon L \to \operatorname {End}_K(V)\) extends uniquely to an associative-algebra homomorphism \(\tilde\rho \colon U(L) \to \operatorname {End}_K(V)\) with \(\tilde\rho \circ \iota = \rho \).
