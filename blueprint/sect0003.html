Caveats

This chapter is not part of the theorem dependency graph above. It collects things worth knowing about the current state of Mathlib relevant to this blueprint: gaps this project fills, gaps it doesn’t, and available infrastructure it doesn’t yet use.

The Casimir element does not exist in Mathlib.

Checked with a case-insensitive search of the entire Mathlib tree for “Casimir”: zero hits. Everything in Chapter 3 (basis-dependent construction, change-of-basis invariance, centrality via the universal enveloping algebra) is new relative to Mathlib, not merely restated in a different form.

No exceptional low-rank Lie algebra isomorphisms.

Mathlib has a genuine general construction of \(\mathfrak {so}(n)\) as skew-symmetric matrices (LieAlgebra.Orthogonal.so, with the equivalent “indefinite form” presentation so’, covering \(\mathfrak {so}(3)\) and \(\mathfrak {so}(4)\) as the low-rank cases of types \(B_1\) and \(D_2\)), and separately the general skew-adjoint-endomorphism construction (Mathlib.Algebra.Lie.SkewAdjoint) it is built from. But we found no proof anywhere of the classical exceptional isomorphisms \(\mathfrak {so}(3)\cong \mathfrak {sl}_2\) or \(\mathfrak {so}(4)\cong \mathfrak {sl}_2\oplus \mathfrak {sl}_2\) (\(B_1 \cong A_1\), \(D_2\cong A_1\times A_1\)) — we checked the root-system and Dynkin-diagram files (CartanMatrix.lean, Irreducible.lean, even the dedicated RootSystem/Finite/G2.lean for the \(G_2\) exceptional type) and found nothing for rank \(\le 2\). Theorem 48 and the \(\vec I,\vec K\) construction in §5.2 therefore prove these isomorphisms ad hoc, computationally, rather than by invoking a general theorem, because no such general theorem is available to invoke.

Mathlib already has the concrete Lie ring structure this project needs for \(\mathfrak {so}(3)\), unused.

Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.CrossProduct packages Fin 3 → R with the cross product as a genuine LieRing (Cross.lieRing), with antisymmetry (cross_anticomm) and the Jacobi identity (jacobi_cross) already proved, and the cross product itself is bilinear by construction (it is built as a LinearMap.mk₂, so Theorem 54 would be free). Neither AngularMomentumRepresentation (§4.1) nor the Levi-Civita-based crossComponent (§5.1) currently uses this: angular momentum is presented as three operators satisfying axiomatized commutation relations, rather than as a representation of this concrete, already-available Lie ring. Routing Chapters 4–5 through Cross.lieRing instead is a live simplification opportunity, not attempted in this pass.

Schur’s lemma exists in Mathlib at two levels, neither directly reusable yet.

Mathlib.RingTheory.SimpleModule.Basic gives the division-ring level (bijective_or_eq_zero, Module.End.instDivisionRing) but stops there — no algebraically-closed-field “it’s a scalar” step. That step exists in full generality in Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Preadditive.Schur (endomorphism_simple_eq_smul_id), for any \(\Bbbk \)-linear category with kernels, algebraically closed \(\Bbbk \), finite-dimensional hom spaces — exactly Theorem 16’s statement, one level of abstraction up. The obstruction to reusing it directly is that Mathlib has no bundled category of Lie modules (no LieModuleCat, unlike ModuleCat or FGModuleCat). Group representations do not have this problem: Mathlib.RepresentationTheory.FDRep is a genuine category and imports CategoryTheory.Preadditive.Schur directly to get Schur’s lemma for free (finrank_hom_simple_simple). FDRep is the exact template a LieModuleCat would follow; building it is a well-scoped, plausible Mathlib contribution, but Theorem 16 as it stands takes the direct route (extract an eigenvalue via Module.End.exists_eigenvalue, show the shifted map is a LieModuleHom with nontrivial kernel, conclude by irreducibility) rather than waiting on that infrastructure.

No Clebsch–Gordan or weight-multiplicity machinery.

Checked for any existing tensor-product decomposition or weight-multiplicity result for \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) or semisimple Lie algebra representations (searching for “ClebschGordan”, and for weight-space/tensor-product combinations in Mathlib.Algebra.Lie.Weights): nothing found. Everything in §2.3–§2.4 is new.

The explicit-representation bridge (§1.1, Remark 13) remains unbuilt.

This is the one gap flagged already in Chapter 1 that is worth re-emphasizing here: Chapters 4–5 use LieRepresentation (an explicit morphism into \(\operatorname {End}_K(V)\)) rather than Mathlib’s instance-based LieModule, specifically because Mathlib has no public constructor turning an explicit morphism back into local LieRingModule/LieModule instances. Until that bridge is built, results proved in the generic, instance-based Chapters 1–3 (irreducibility transfer, the classification theorems, Casimir scalars) have to be re-derived or manually transported for the explicit-morphism objects in Chapters 4–5 rather than applied directly.
