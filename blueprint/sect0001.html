Overview

This blueprint lays out a roadmap from the bare notion of a Lie algebra representation to the energy spectrum of the hydrogen atom, formalized on top of Mathlib’s existing Lie theory. It is organized as a dependency graph of theorems and constructions, not of individual small lemmas: each node below corresponds to a genuine mathematical statement, and the graph edges (via uses) record exactly which results feed into which.

Nodes already available in Mathlib are marked with a dark green border in the dependency graph (); nodes already proved in this project are green (); everything else is a target for future formalization work. The chapters mirror the source tree:

  • Representation theory — Representation/Basic.lean, Representation/Schur.lean

  • The Lie algebra \(\mathfrak {sl}_2\) — Sl2/Basic.lean, Sl2/Classification.lean, Sl2/CommutingActions.lean, Sl2/ClebschGordan.lean

  • The Casimir element — Casimir/Basic.lean, Casimir/Centrality.lean

  • Angular momentum — AngularMomentum/Basic.lean, AngularMomentum/Classification.lean

  • The hydrogen atom — Hydrogen/Basic.lean, Hydrogen/Symmetry.lean, Hydrogen/Spectrum.lean

Throughout, \(K\) is a field (eventually specialized to \(\mathbb {C}\)), \(L\) a Lie algebra over \(K\), and modules are finite-dimensional unless stated otherwise.

Scope of Chapters 4–5, and a note on Physlib.

Physlib already has a substantial, mostly-proved analytic treatment of this material — QuantumMechanics/Hydrogen/Basic.lean and .../LaplaceRungeLenzVector.lean define the (regularized) hydrogen Hamiltonian and Runge–Lenz operator on Schwartz space and prove their commutation relations and the identity \(\mathbf{A}^2 = 2m\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{L}^2 + \tfrac 14\hbar ^2(d-1)^2) + \dots \); QuantumMechanics/Operators/AngularMomentum.lean proves the analogous facts for \(\mathbf L\). Its own module documentation lists the bound-state energy spectrum as an open TODO. Chapters 4–5 below are deliberately kept as a decoupled, finite-dimensional algebraic model: they do not depend on Physlib’s Hilbert-space/self-adjoint-operator machinery, and in particular do not attempt the analytic work (essential self-adjointness, finite-dimensionality of eigenspaces) needed to connect a literal Physlib HydrogenAtom to the abstract representation theory here. Wiring the two together — so that this project’s classification theorems discharge Physlib’s open TODO — is future work, not attempted in this pass.
